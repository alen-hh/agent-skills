# Dan Koe Style Guide

To write like Dan Koe, you must master his vocabulary and his method of elevating mundane business advice into profound philosophical insights.

## Core Vocabulary

Use these terms naturally to establish the tone:

* **Signal**: Original thought, deep insight, ideas that resonate on a human level.
* **Slop / Noise**: Generic, AI-generated, or mass-produced content that lacks taste or personal context.
* **The Matrix / The Script**: The conventional path of school, 9-to-5 jobs, and societal expectations.
* **One-Person Business**: A highly leveraged, internet-based business run by a single creator.
* **Specific Knowledge**: Unique skills and insights gained through genuine curiosity and obsession, not formal training.
* **Taste**: The ability to discern quality, developed through exposure and repetition.
* **Mission-based**: Creating content around a personal transformation rather than a rigid, traditional "niche."
* **Future-proof**: Adapting to technological changes (like AI) by leaning into human uniqueness.

## The "Average vs. Dan Koe" Transformation

The key to this style is taking a standard piece of advice and passing it through a philosophical lens.

### Example 1: Finding a Niche

**Average Advice**:
"You need to pick a specific niche so you can target a specific audience and sell them a product."

**Dan Koe Style**:
"Topic-based niches are a trap. 
They box you in. They force you to talk about things you no longer care about.
If you want to survive the next decade, you don't need a niche. You need a mission.
You are the niche. Your curiosities, your struggles, and the problems you've solved are your specific knowledge.
Don't build authority in a topic. Lead people toward a transformation."

### Example 2: Using AI for Content

**Average Advice**:
"Use ChatGPT to write your blog posts faster so you can post every day and grow your audience."

**Dan Koe Style**:
"AI made everyone fast. 
Anyone can generate a 'how-to' thread in seconds. Volume is no longer an advantage; it's just noise.
The barrier to entry has dropped to zero, but the skill cap has never been higher.
If you hand AI a generic prompt, you get generic slop. 
Your advantage isn't doing what AI can't do. Your advantage is doing what only *you* would think to do with AI. 
Pass your taste, your specific knowledge, and your vision to the machine. The labor is distributed, but the taste remains yours."

### Example 3: Working Hard

**Average Advice**:
"You need to hustle and grind for 80 hours a week if you want to be successful."

**Dan Koe Style**:
"The 40-hour workweek is a mechanical model for a digital age.
We've been trained to equate labor with value. 
But fortunes require leverage, not just sweat. 
When you build a one-person business, you aren't trading time for money. You are building assets—code and media—that work while you sleep.
Focus for 2 to 4 hours a day on deep, meaningful work. Let the internet do the rest."
